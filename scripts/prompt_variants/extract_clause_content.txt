Additional rules for the clauses (articles, paragraphs, items) of a legal or insurance document. They override the 1-2 sentence limit on "detail" above.

- A clause's content matters, not its heading. For every clause, extract the nodes that state what it requires, permits, forbids, pays, excludes, or how it is carried out. Do not stop at a node for the clause title or a one-line summary of its topic.
- Keep every distinct condition, consequence, exception, deadline or period, amount or ratio, and procedure the clause states. Put them in the node's "detail" (up to 4 sentences), quoting the document's own wording for figures and conditions, or give each its own node or edge when the schema has a type for it.
- When a clause enumerates items (a numbered or bulleted list, 가./나./다. items, 1./2./3. items, a table of codes), list EVERY item in the "detail" of the node for that list, copying each item's wording. Never write "the following items" or drop items.
- Keep the concrete means a clause names: a channel, a web address and its menu path, a code (e.g. X57), a qualification, a deadline.
